# exp004 — The matcher is most confident exactly where it is making things up

**Record of record:** [`experiments/exp004_real_data_transfer/findings.md`](../../experiments/exp004_real_data_transfer/findings.md)
· **Issue:** [#6](https://github.com/visgraf/active-stereo/issues/6)
· **Run:** `exp004-20260818T213856-640b00e`

---

## The question

Everything this project had measured came from a stimulus we built ourselves.
Random dots in exp001. Rendered charts in exp003. Both were designed so that a
failure would have an identifiable cause — and both, by construction, were made by
the same people who wrote the thing being tested.

[exp003's briefing](exp003-appearance-and-matching.md) wrote down the debt at the
time: *"A test chart is not a scene. Attribution was bought at the cost of realism;
these results transfer to real imagery only insofar as real surfaces resemble flat
painted patches."*

So: do they?

## What we tested against

The [Middlebury 2014 datasets](https://vision.middlebury.edu/stereo/data/scenes2014/)
— photographs of real indoor scenes, with depth measured by a structured-light
scanner. Ten of them, chosen and written down before anything was downloaded.

Three things about this corpus turned out to matter more than expected.

**Their camera is our camera.** Middlebury publish a calibration constant called
`doffs` — the offset between the two lenses' principal points. Working through the
algebra, it is *exactly* the vergence term this framework already had. Not
approximately: identically. A physical rig, built and calibrated by people with no
stake in our modelling choices, is described by the model
[ADR-0007](../decisions/0007-offaxis-not-toein.md) picked. That is the closest
thing to an outside opinion this project has received.

**Real ground truth has holes.** A scanner sees nothing on dark, glancing or thin
surfaces, and leaves 0.6% to 10.7% of each frame simply unknown. Every stimulus
before this one knew the depth of every pixel, because it had placed them. Those
holes are not occlusion and not a field-of-view limit — they are a gap in *our
knowledge*, and charging them to occlusion would have inflated the single number
this project argues from most. They now get their own mask
([ADR-0011](../decisions/0011-ground-truth-known-mask.md)).

**Each scene ships its own photometry experiment.** Alongside the normal right
image, Middlebury include one shot at a **different exposure** and one under
**different lighting** — same camera, same geometry, same ground truth. That is
exp003's "hold the geometry fixed and change only the appearance" design, handed to
us for free, on real photographs.

## What we predicted

Three claims, with numeric falsifiers, registered before the run.

1. **The calibration asymmetry transfers.** exp003 found that when a matcher has
   *no* evidence (a blank wall) it says so loudly, and when it has *false* evidence
   (a half-occlusion) it stays calm. That should still hold on photographs.
2. **It is worse than "calm".** From a first look at one scene: variance in
   occlusions might be *lower* than in genuinely matched regions. Flagged as
   generated by that peek, and therefore weaker evidence than a real prediction.
3. **Photometric mismatch fabricates evidence.** Changing exposure or lighting
   breaks brightness constancy, so the matcher should keep answering and be wrong —
   error up, coverage flat.

## What happened

### The behaviour transfers almost exactly

Block matching fills **82.6%** of genuine half-occlusions with a confident answer.
On random dots, exp001 measured **79.6%**. Two stimulus families with nothing
whatsoever in common, three percentage points apart. Whatever this failure is, it
is not an artefact of synthetic stimuli.

### But the *uncertainty* is worse than exp003 could see

exp003 measured a 5× variance inflation in occlusions and called it insufficient.
On photographs the number is **0.32×** — the variance is not merely too small, it is
**lower** than on pixels the matcher genuinely matched, in seven of eight scenes.

> The matcher's most confident answers are the ones with no correspondent at all.

That matters because of what happens downstream. Depth cues are fused in inverse
proportion to variance, so a confident measurement dominates the fusion. The
estimates being weighted most heavily are the fabricated ones.

### And we found out why

The pre-registered worry was that Middlebury's occluded regions might just be dark
or smooth, in which case this would be exp003's *texture* effect in disguise. So
occluded pixels were split by how much local contrast they sit in.

The check came back inverted:

| Where the occlusion is | Reported variance | vs. matched |
|---|---|---|
| Low contrast — shadowed, smooth | 11,447 px² | 2.95× |
| **High contrast — crisp, well-lit edges** | **395 px²** | **0.12×** |

Low-contrast occlusions behave exactly as exp003 described: the matcher is unsure,
says so, and the answer is safely discounted. **The danger is the high-contrast
ones** — 8.3× more confident than a genuine match on the median scene, up to 34.5×
on the worst.

And that has a mechanical explanation, which is what makes it actionable rather
than just alarming. A half-occluded pixel next to a strong edge produces a *sharp,
unambiguous* minimum in the matching cost — at the wrong disparity. The variance is
derived from how sharp that minimum is. Sharpness gets read as precision. The
estimate is confidently, precisely wrong, and nothing in a single cost curve could
tell it apart from a correct match.

The pixels this happens to are the crisp, well-lit, obviously-textured ones. Which
is to say: the pixels a gaze policy would deliberately choose to look at.

### The third prediction was simply wrong

Changing exposure or lighting did not make the matcher confidently wrong. It made
it **stop answering** — coverage fell by 47.6 points on block matching, against a
falsifier that said a 10-point drop would already refute the prediction.

That is the *withheld*-evidence signature, not the fabricated one. Recorded as
falsified rather than reinterpreted.

It is also the safer failure, and the two matchers split instructively. SGBM mostly
just declines. Block matching declines *and* the answers it keeps are 44× worse —
so there is fabrication here too, accompanied by mass abstention rather than
replaced by it.

## A number that changes an earlier recommendation

exp001's practical conclusion was to prefer SGBM, because it declined where block
matching guessed: 9.0% against 79.6%.

| | random dots (exp001) | photographs (exp004) |
|---|---|---|
| block matching | 79.6% | 82.6% |
| **SGBM** | **9.0%** | **53.6%** |

Block matching transfers. **SGBM's advantage does not** — on photographs it fills
more than half of all half-occlusions. The matcher we recommended for its honesty
was mostly being honest about random dots.

This was not something we set out to test; it surfaced while checking the draft
against exp001. It is filed as [#10](https://github.com/visgraf/active-stereo/issues/10)
and needs its own experiment before it counts as a result.

There is a second problem with the same matcher. SGBM reports a **constant**
uncertainty — the same number for every pixel it answers. So two of exp004's
verdict cells were vacuous: its variance test could not fail, and its inverse could
not pass. [ADR-0005](../decisions/0005-uncertainty-first-class.md) requires every
estimator to return a variance. It never required that variance to mean anything,
and in four experiments nobody had checked.

## What this changes

1. **Build occlusion detection, not a texture gate.** exp003 already argued this;
   exp004 sharpens it. Occluded estimates are not under-trusted, they are
   *over*-trusted relative to correct ones, and the exposure concentrates where the
   image looks easiest. [#7](https://github.com/visgraf/active-stereo/issues/7)
2. **Cost curvature cannot be rescued by tuning.** It answers "how well localised
   is this match, *given that a match exists*". The question that matters here is
   whether one exists at all, and curvature has no way to express it.
   [#8](https://github.com/visgraf/active-stereo/issues/8)
3. **A constant is not an uncertainty model.**
   [#9](https://github.com/visgraf/active-stereo/issues/9)
4. **For the paper**, one word changes and it matters. Not "poorly calibrated for
   fabricated evidence" — ***anti*-calibrated**. Poorly calibrated suggests a number
   that needs rescaling. This one needs replacing.

## How the honesty was kept

Two things worth stating, because both are places this could have gone wrong.

**We looked at the data twice before the confirmatory run.** Once while checking
the loader worked, once while checking the runner's plumbing. Both were disclosed
on the tracking issue *before* the real run, so the timestamps prove no threshold
was adjusted afterwards, and both scenes were dropped from every reported average.
The falsifier for the peek-derived hypothesis stayed at "3 or more scenes" rather
than being rescaled when the held-out set shrank from nine to eight — a threshold
that tracks the sample size is a threshold chosen after seeing the sample.

**Three mistakes were caught before the numbers were visible**, which is the only
reason they are corrections and not retractions: a search range set too short for
two scenes (one of them held out), a statistic that ignored the paired design, and
a falsifier that could not fail for one of the matchers.

One was caught *after*: the high-contrast effect was first written up as "up to
12×" when it is 34.5×. Building the figure that plots all eight scenes is what
exposed it. The error understated the finding.

## Caveats

- Ten scenes of indoor, static, tabletop imagery is not XR content, and eight
  held-out scenes is a small sample.
- We downsampled the images ourselves by a factor of 3. That is a blur, and it
  removes exactly the fine texture exp003 found decisive.
- Contrast is measured on the photograph, because a photograph has no albedo pass —
  the very conflation exp003 warned about. The split is coarser than exp003's.
- There is no independent occlusion ground truth in this corpus. Ours reproduces
  Middlebury's own generator exactly, which validates the code and not the
  threshold inside it.
- Block matching is a deliberately simple reference implementation. Its absolute
  error is not the finding.
